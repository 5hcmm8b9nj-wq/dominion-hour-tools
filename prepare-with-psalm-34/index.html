BEFORE YOU BEGIN

Who Wrote Psalm 34?

Author: King David.    Approximate date: around 1062 B.C., during the years David was fleeing King Saul.

THE SETTING
1 Samuel 21:10–22:2

David was on the run for his life. King Saul was hunting him. With nowhere left to go, David fled into enemy territory — the Philistine city of Gath — and stood before King Achish (also called Abimelech, a royal title). Recognized as the giant-killer, David feared for his life and pretended to be insane. Achish drove him out. David escaped to the cave of Adullam. There, in that cave, Psalm 34 was born.

WHAT WAS GOING ON
A song written from a cave

David was hunted, homeless, hungry, and misunderstood. He had just faked madness to save his life. Yet from that cave he lifted his voice and wrote one of the most triumphant psalms in Scripture. Psalm 34 is a thanksgiving song — David's public testimony that God delivered him when he had no one else to run to. It is a psalm born in trouble and sung in victory.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR YOU
What David wrote, you can walk in

Every promise in Psalm 34 was tested in David's life before it was written down. He is not quoting theory — he is testifying. The angel that encamped around him (v.7), the goodness he tasted (v.8), the no-lack promise (v.10) — David lived every one of these. As you prepare this month, you are stepping into the same covenant David stood on.

THE FULL PSALM

Psalm 34  —  King James Version

Verses 6–12 are your daily reading for this month.

  1. 1I will bless the LORD at all times: his praise shall continually be in my mouth.
  2. 2My soul shall make her boast in the LORD: the humble shall hear thereof, and be glad.
  3. 3O magnify the LORD with me, and let us exalt his name together.
  4. 4I sought the LORD, and he heard me, and delivered me from all my fears.
  5. 5They looked unto him, and were lightened: and their faces were not ashamed.
  6. 6This poor man cried, and the LORD heard him, and saved him out of all his troubles.
  7. 7The angel of the LORD encampeth round about them that fear him, and delivereth them.
  8. 8O taste and see that the LORD is good: blessed is the man that trusteth in him.
  9. 9O fear the LORD, ye his saints: for there is no want to them that fear him.
  10. 10The young lions do lack, and suffer hunger: but they that seek the LORD shall not want any good thing.
  11. 11Come, ye children, hearken unto me: I will teach you the fear of the LORD.
  1. 12What man is he that desireth life, and loveth many days, that he may see good?
  2. 13Keep thy tongue from evil, and thy lips from speaking guile.
  3. 14Depart from evil, and do good; seek peace, and pursue it.
  4. 15The eyes of the LORD are upon the righteous, and his ears are open unto their cry.
  5. 16The face of the LORD is against them that do evil, to cut off the remembrance of them from the earth.
  6. 17The righteous cry, and the LORD heareth, and delivereth them out of all their troubles.
  7. 18The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.
  8. 19Many are the afflictions of the righteous: but the LORD delivereth him out of them all.
  9. 20He keepeth all his bones: not one of them is broken.
  10. 21Evil shall slay the wicked: and they that hate the righteous shall be desolate.
  11. 22The LORD redeemeth the soul of his servants: and none of them that trust in him shall be desolate.
“Oh, taste and see that the Lord is good.” — PSALM 34:8
