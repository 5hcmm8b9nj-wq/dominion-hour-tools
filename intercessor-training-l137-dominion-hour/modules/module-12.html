The Intercessor's Life — Character, Aftercare, Longevity

Luke 18:1 (KJV) — "And he spake a parable unto them to this end, that men ought always to pray, and not to faint."

Module objectives

By the end of Module 12, you will be able to:

  1. Guard the intercessor's heart — humility, hiddenness, holiness.
  2. Practise spiritual aftercare — Word, worship, rest, fellowship, medical/pastoral help when needed.
  3. Stay hidden in Christ — no self-promotion, no showmanship.
  4. Finish the race well.

Opening — the ministry standard

Before we teach, we obey the flow that Pastor Kiky Ocansey and Prophet Prince Amo David keep in every service:

  1. Praise and worship
  2. Thanksgiving
  3. Confession of sins
  4. Forgive others
  5. Invite the Holy Spirit
  6. Teaching
  7. Prayers and declarations
  8. Sealing with the blood of Jesus

Do this every time you open this module, whether alone or in a group. Do not skip. Do not reorder.

Teaching

1. Always to pray, and not to faint

Luke 18:1 (KJV)

And he spake a parable unto them to this end, that men ought always to pray, and not to faint.

Jesus attached two commands to the parable of the persistent widow: pray always, and do not faint. Intercession is not a sprint; it is a lifelong walk. The intercessor's crown is not given at the beginning of the race. It is given at the end.

2. Not weary in well doing

Galatians 6:9 (KJV)

And let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not.

Every intercessor comes to seasons of weariness. Do not be surprised. Do not be ashamed. Do not withdraw from the altar in silence. Confess weariness to the Father, receive a fresh coal from the altar (Isaiah 6:6), and keep going. In due season, you shall reap.

3. Keep the body under

1 Corinthians 9:27 (KJV)

But I keep under my body, and bring it into subjection: lest that by any means, when I have preached to others, I myself should be a castaway.

The intercessor is a disciplined athlete of the Spirit. This includes:

  • Adequate sleep — rest is not laziness
  • Real food — the intercessor is not a spirit; the body must be nourished
  • Movement — walk, stretch, exercise as you are able
  • Medical care where needed — do not treat a chemical imbalance as a demon, and do not treat a demon as a chemical imbalance; seek discernment
  • Pastoral care — never carry a heavy prophetic word alone; report to your covering leadership
  • Boundaries — say no to what is not your assignment

4. Wait upon the Lord

Isaiah 40:31 (KJV)

But they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles; they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint.

Renewed strength comes from waiting, not from hurrying. The intercessor learns to wait — in silence, in the Word, in worship, in refilling — before every fresh assignment.

5. Hidden in Christ

Colossians 3:3 (KJV)

For ye are dead, and your life is hid with Christ in God.

Matthew 6:6 (KJV)

But thou, when thou prayest, enter into thy closet, and when thou hast shut thy door, pray to thy Father which is in secret; and thy Father which seeth in secret shall reward thee openly.

The greatest intercessors are the least visible. Their life is hid with Christ in God. They do not post prayer selfies. They do not broadcast their fasts. They do not weaponise prophecy to control others. They enter the closet, shut the door, and let the Father who sees in secret reward openly.

6. Aftercare protocol

After heavy intercession or a deliverance session:

  1. Seal with the blood of Jesus
  2. Wash — a physical shower is a helpful symbol of cleansing (not a ritual)
  3. Eat and hydrate
  4. Read the Word — replace what was poured out
  5. Worship — soft, quiet, prolonged
  6. Rest — sleep protects the intercessor
  7. Report — brief your intercessor coordinator on the assignment
  8. Speak to a pastor or Christian counsellor if the burden is unusually heavy
  9. Seek qualified medical support for any persistent physical or emotional symptoms

7. Commissioning

Upon completion of the 12 modules, all assignments, and the personal Rule of Life, the intercessor is presented to pastoral leadership for confirmation and commissioning. This is not graduation from prayer — it is enlistment into a lifelong company.

Welcome to the intercessors' company of L137 Deliverance Ministry International and Dominion Hour.

Prayer points

Pray each point aloud. Take at least three minutes on each.

  1. Father, I thank You for the grace to endure to the end, in the name of Jesus. (Matthew 24:13)
  2. Lord, teach me to always pray, and never to faint, in the name of Jesus. (Luke 18:1)
  3. Father, deliver me from weariness in well doing, in the name of Jesus. (Galatians 6:9)
  4. Lord, keep me humble, hidden, and holy, in the name of Jesus. (Micah 6:8)
  5. Father, guard my heart against pride, vainglory, and spiritual pretence, in the name of Jesus. (Proverbs 4:23)
  6. Lord, teach me to rest without guilt, in the name of Jesus. (Mark 6:31)
  7. Father, give me wisdom to eat, sleep, and move in a way that sustains my body, in the name of Jesus. (1 Corinthians 6:19-20)
  8. Lord, connect me to godly counsel and pastoral covering for aftercare, in the name of Jesus. (Proverbs 11:14)
  9. Father, help me distinguish spiritual burdens from medical needs, and seek help wisely, in the name of Jesus.
  10. Lord, let my life be hid with Christ in God, in the name of Jesus. (Colossians 3:3)
  11. Father, hide me from every temptation to broadcast, boast, or perform, in the name of Jesus. (Matthew 6:6)
  12. Lord, commission me for a lifetime of intercession, in the name of Jesus.
  13. Father, at the end of my race, let me hear "Well done, good and faithful servant," in the name of Jesus. (Matthew 25:21)

Sealing

I cover this prayer, my mind, my body, my family, and my assignment with the precious blood of Jesus Christ. Every arrow returned, every gate sealed, every ministry protected, in the name of Jesus. Amen.

Reflection questions

  1. Where are you most tempted to faint right now?
  2. How do you practically care for your body in the middle of heavy intercession?
  3. Do you have a pastor or trusted leader to whom you report your prayer burdens?
  4. Are you hidden in Christ, or are you slowly building a personal platform?
  5. What is your written Rule of Life for the next 12 months as an intercessor?

Weekly assignment — Write your personal Rule of Life

Complete the training by writing a personal Rule of Life for intercession. Include:

  1. Daily rhythms — altar hour, Bible time, prayer slots, family prayer, Sabbath
  2. Weekly rhythms — corporate service, small group, longer prayer window
  3. Monthly rhythms — extended fast, personal retreat, review of prophetic journal
  4. Annual rhythms — L137 / Dominion Hour Intercessors Camp attendance, personal medical check-up, pastoral review
  5. Boundaries — what you will say no to (media, gossip, night-owl habits, over-committing)
  6. Aftercare — how you will replenish after heavy assignments
  7. Mentorship — who you report to, who you learn from, who you pray with
  8. Accountability — who you meet with, how often, and what you share

Sign it. Date it. Submit it to pastoral leadership for confirmation of your commissioning as an intercessor of L137 Deliverance Ministry International and Dominion Hour.

Closing prayer

Father, in the name of Jesus, I answer the call for life. Teach me to always pray, and not to faint. Keep my body strong, my mind clear, my heart tender, my mouth clean. Hide me in Christ. Cover me with the blood of Jesus. Position me among the standing intercessors of L137 and Dominion Hour. And at the end of my race, let me hear, "Well done, good and faithful servant." In Jesus' mighty name, Amen.
