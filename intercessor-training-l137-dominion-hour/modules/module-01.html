Teaching

1. What is intercession?

Intercession is standing in the gap between a holy God and a broken people, and pleading the case on the ground of the finished work of Jesus Christ.

The word "intercession" carries the picture of a person who meets with someone, on behalf of someone else, to move a matter forward. The intercessor is not the judge and not the accused — the intercessor is the one who stands between.

Personal prayer says, "Lord, help me." Intercession says, "Lord, help them." Both are commanded. Both are welcomed. But intercession requires a further step: the intercessor lays down personal comfort and stands where the fire is.

2. God is looking for the intercessor

Ezekiel 22:30 (KJV)

And I sought for a man among them, that should make up the hedge, and stand in the gap before me for the land, that I should not destroy it: but I found none.

Notice three things:

  • God is searching. He does not draft. He seeks and He calls.
  • The gap is real. There is a breach — a place where the hedge is broken and the enemy has legal access.
  • One person can shift the outcome. God said, "I should not destroy it." One standing intercessor changed the divine verdict.
Isaiah 59:16 (KJV)

And he saw that there was no man, and wondered that there was no intercessor: therefore his arm brought salvation unto him; and his righteousness, it sustained him.

God is astonished when He looks and finds no intercessor. He is honoured when He looks and finds you.

3. The pattern of Abraham — Genesis 18

Genesis 18:23 (KJV)

And Abraham drew near, and said, Wilt thou also destroy the righteous with the wicked?

Abraham stood before the Lord and negotiated for Sodom. He did not lecture God. He appealed to the character of God — "Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?" (Genesis 18:25).

The intercessor's foundation is not human passion. It is the character of God, the promises of God, and the finished work of Jesus Christ.

Abraham teaches us four postures:

  1. Draw near — the intercessor comes close, does not stand at a distance.
  2. Speak boldly, speak humbly — "Behold now, I have taken upon me to speak unto the Lord, which am but dust and ashes." (Genesis 18:27)
  3. Persist — Abraham asked six times.
  4. Trust the outcome to God — when Abraham stopped, God stopped. The intercessor does not have to fix everything; the intercessor only has to stand.

4. Intercession is commanded, not optional

1 Timothy 2:1–4 (KJV)

I exhort therefore, that, first of all, supplications, prayers, intercessions, and giving of thanks, be made for all men; For kings, and for all that are in authority; that we may lead a quiet and peaceable life in all godliness and honesty. For this is good and acceptable in the sight of God our Saviour; Who will have all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth.

Paul places intercession first of all. It is the New Testament believer's frontline assignment. It is not for a special elite — it is the calling of every born-again child of God.

Prayer points — The Call

Pray each point aloud. Take at least three minutes on each.

  1. Father, I thank You for choosing me before the foundation of the world, in the name of Jesus. (Ephesians 1:4)
  2. Father, I repent of every time I heard Your call and refused it, in the name of Jesus. (1 John 1:9)
  3. Father, I forgive every person who wounded me and caused me to withdraw from prayer, in the name of Jesus. (Matthew 6:14)
  4. Holy Spirit, I invite You now — teach me to intercede, in the name of Jesus. (Romans 8:26)
  5. Lord, open my ears to hear Your call clearly today, in the name of Jesus. (1 Samuel 3:9)
  6. Lord, purge my lips with the live coal from Your altar, in the name of Jesus. (Isaiah 6:6–7)
  7. Lord, I say with Isaiah: "Here am I, send me" — commission me now, in the name of Jesus. (Isaiah 6:8)
  8. Father, position me in the gap where the hedge is broken in my family, in the name of Jesus. (Ezekiel 22:30)
  9. Father, position me in the gap for L137 Deliverance Ministry International and Pastor Kiky Ocansey, in the name of Jesus.
  10. Father, position me in the gap for Dominion Hour and Prophet Prince Amo David, in the name of Jesus.
  11. Father, deliver me from every spirit of slumber and prayerlessness, in the name of Jesus. (Romans 11:8)
  12. Father, kindle the fire of intercession on the altar of my heart, and let it never go out, in the name of Jesus. (Leviticus 6:13)

Reflection questions

  1. When did you first feel drawn to pray for other people, not just yourself?
  2. What has held you back from answering the call to intercession?
  3. Where is the gap in your family, your workplace, your church, your nation?
  4. Whose face is God putting before you as an intercession assignment right now?
  5. What does "Here am I, Lord — send me" cost you this week?

Weekly assignment — Write your "Here am I, Lord" letter

On a single sheet of paper (or a document), address the Lord directly and write:

  • The date you answered the call
  • The gaps you sense you are called to stand in (family, ministry, workplace, city, nation)
  • The pastors and ministries you are volunteering to cover in prayer
  • The time slot you are committing to (recommended: a fixed 30-minute window daily — many intercessors use 5 AM as their personal altar hour)
  • Your signature

Sign it. Date it. Read it aloud before the Lord. Keep it in your Bible or your prayer altar space. This is your personal Ezekiel 22:30 covenant.

Closing prayer

Father, in the name of Jesus Christ, I thank You for the call to intercession. I answer today. I am not qualified by myself — I am qualified by the blood of Jesus, by the finished work of the Cross, and by the indwelling Holy Spirit. Position me in the gap. Teach me to stand. Fill my mouth with Your Word. Fill my spirit with Your fire. Cover me, my family, my pastors, and my ministry assignment with the precious blood of Jesus. I will not hold my peace. I will not rest. Here am I, Lord — send me. In Jesus' mighty name I pray. Amen.
