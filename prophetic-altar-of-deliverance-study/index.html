The Three Foundations

Three altar Scriptures form the spine of the teaching: the promise of deliverance, the command to throw down, and the work of repair.

Foundation 1 · The Promise

Obadiah 1:17 — Deliverance, holiness, possession

17But upon mount Zion shall be deliverance, and there shall be holiness; and the house of Jacob shall possess their possessions.

Obadiah 1:17 · King James Version
See the three things promised in one verse

Deliverance — a place of escape and rescue is named. Holiness — the delivered place is also a set-apart place. Possession — what belonged to the house of Jacob returns to the house of Jacob.

The order in the verse matters: deliverance comes first, holiness follows, and possession follows holiness.

Study Note — what “possess their possessions” means

Obadiah is written against Edom, who profited while Jacob was plundered (Obadiah 1:11–14). Verse 17 is the turn: what was carried off is not merely replaced, it is repossessed. The word picture is of an owner walking back into property that was always legally his.

Answer for yourself: what is the “possession” in your own life?

Name it plainly before you pray Prayer Point 8. Write it in the My Notes section.

Foundation 2 · The Command

Judges 6:25–26 — Throw down, then build

25And it came to pass the same night, that the LORD said unto him, Take thy father’s young bullock, even the second bullock of seven years old, and throw down the altar of Baal that thy father hath, and cut down the grove that is by it:

26And build an altar unto the LORD thy God upon the top of this rock, in the ordered place, and take the second bullock, and offer a burnt sacrifice with the wood of the grove which thou shalt cut down.

Judges 6:25–26 · King James Version
Read what happened next — Judges 6:27–32

27Then Gideon took ten men of his servants, and did as the LORD had said unto him: and so it was, because he feared his father’s household, and the men of the city, that he could not do it by day, that he did it by night.

28And when the men of the city arose early in the morning, behold, the altar of Baal was cast down, and the grove was cut down that was by it, and the second bullock was offered upon the altar that was built.

29And they said one to another, Who hath done this thing? And when they enquired and asked, they said, Gideon the son of Joash hath done this thing.

30Then the men of the city said unto Joash, Bring out thy son, that he may die: because he hath cast down the altar of Baal, and because he hath cut down the grove that was by it.

31And Joash said unto all that stood against him, Will ye plead for Baal? will ye save him? he that will plead for him, let him be put to death whilst it is yet morning: if he be a god, let him plead for himself, because one hath cast down his altar.

32Therefore on that day he called him Jerubbaal, saying, Let Baal plead against him, because he hath thrown down his altar.

Judges 6:27–32 · King James Version
Four things the LORD told Gideon to do

1. Take the bullock. 2. Throw down the altar of Baal that thy father hath. 3. Cut down the grove beside it. 4. Build an altar unto the LORD and offer the sacrifice using the wood of the very grove he cut down.

Study Note — “the altar of Baal that thy father hath”

The altar Gideon was sent to destroy was not built by an enemy nation. It stood in his own father’s compound and he had grown up beside it. Scripture makes the inherited altar the first assignment, before Gideon ever faced Midian.

Notice too that nothing was left standing empty: the ground that carried Baal’s altar carried the LORD’s altar the same night, and the grove’s own wood fed the fire.

Foundation 3 · The Repair

1 Kings 18:30 — He repaired the altar that was broken down

30And Elijah said unto all the people, Come near unto me. And all the people came near unto him. And he repaired the altar of the LORD that was broken down.

1 Kings 18:30 · King James Version
Read the whole account — 1 Kings 18:31–39

31And Elijah took twelve stones, according to the number of the tribes of the sons of Jacob, unto whom the word of the LORD came, saying, Israel shall be thy name:

32And with the stones he built an altar in the name of the LORD: and he made a trench about the altar, as great as would contain two measures of seed.

33And he put the wood in order, and cut the bullock in pieces, and laid him on the wood, and said, Fill four barrels with water, and pour it on the burnt sacrifice, and on the wood.

34And he said, Do it the second time. And they did it the second time. And he said, Do it the third time. And they did it the third time.

35And the water ran round about the altar; and he filled the trench also with water.

36And it came to pass at the time of the offering of the evening sacrifice, that Elijah the prophet came near, and said, LORD God of Abraham, Isaac, and of Israel, let it be known this day that thou art God in Israel, and that I am thy servant, and that I have done all these things at thy word.

37Hear me, O LORD, hear me, that this people may know that thou art the LORD God, and that thou hast turned their heart back again.

38Then the fire of the LORD fell, and consumed the burnt sacrifice, and the wood, and the stones, and the dust, and licked up the water that was in the trench.

39And when all the people saw it, they fell on their faces: and they said, The LORD, he is the God; the LORD, he is the God.

1 Kings 18:31–39 · King James Version
Study Note — repair came before fire

The fire in verse 38 fell on an altar that had first been repaired in verse 30 and rebuilt with twelve stones in verses 31–32. Elijah did not ask for fire on rubble. He called the people near, restored what was broken down, set the sacrifice in order, and then prayed.

Compare the three foundations side by side

Obadiah 1:17 — deliverance is promised on the mountain of the LORD.
Judges 6:25–26 — the wrong altar is thrown down and the right one is built in its place.
1 Kings 18:30 — the LORD’s own altar, broken down through neglect, is repaired before the fire falls.

Together they describe demolition, construction, and restoration.

Dagon & the Fallen Idol

Prayer points 3 and 4 quote 1 Samuel 5. Here is the passage in full, in the King James Version and in the wording Prophet Prince used.

Full passage

1 Samuel 5:1–5 (KJV)

1And the Philistines took the ark of God, and brought it from Ebenezer unto Ashdod.

2When the Philistines took the ark of God, they brought it into the house of Dagon, and set it by Dagon.

3And when they of Ashdod arose early on the morrow, behold, Dagon was fallen upon his face to the earth before the ark of the LORD. And they took Dagon, and set him in his place again.

4And when they arose early on the morrow morning, behold, Dagon was fallen upon his face to the ground before the ark of the LORD; and the head of Dagon and both the palms of his hands were cut off upon the threshold; only the stump of Dagon was left to him.

5Therefore neither the priests of Dagon, nor any that come into Dagon’s house, tread on the threshold of Dagon in Ashdod unto this day.

1 Samuel 5:1–5 · King James Version

As quoted in the prayer points

Point 3 — “And when the people of Ashdod arose early in the morning, there was Dagon, fallen on its face to the earth before the ark of the Lord.”

Point 4 — “So they took Dagon and set it in its place again. And when they arose early the following day, there was Dagon, fallen on its face to the ground before the ark of the Lord. The head of Dagon and both the palms of its hands were broken off on the threshold; only Dagon’s torso was left of it.”

The prayer points quote the New King James Version; the KJV renders the same clause “only the stump of Dagon was left to him.”

Work through the passage

Four observations to uncover

Who moved Dagon the first time, and who moved him the second?

Verse 3 — the men of Ashdod found him fallen and they took Dagon, and set him in his place again. Verse 4 — he was fallen again, but this time with the head and both palms cut off. Nobody is recorded as setting him back after that.

What exactly was cut off?

“The head of Dagon and both the palms of his hands were cut off upon the threshold.” Head and hands — and what remained was only the stump.

What did the ark do to bring this about?

Nothing is recorded. There is no battle, no prophet, no priest, no prayer in the passage. The ark was simply set beside Dagon, and Dagon fell before it.

Study Note — the threshold in verse 5

The breaking happened “upon the threshold,” the doorway of Dagon’s own house. Verse 5 records that from that day the priests of Dagon would not step on that threshold — the place of their god’s defeat became a place they avoided.

How this feeds prayer points 3 and 4

Point 3: “Let altars erected against me fall into pieces like Dagon in Jesus name.”

Point 4: “Father, break the head of every Dagon and chop off its palms in Jesus’ name. Let them identify Dagon only by his torso.”

Crying Against the Altar

Prayer point 10 sends you to 1 Kings 13:2 — the man of God who did not argue with the king, but addressed the altar itself.

Full passage

1 Kings 13:1–5 (KJV)

1And, behold, there came a man of God out of Judah by the word of the LORD unto Bethel: and Jeroboam stood by the altar to burn incense.

2And he cried against the altar in the word of the LORD, and said, O altar, altar, thus saith the LORD; Behold, a child shall be born unto the house of David, Josiah by name; and upon thee shall he offer the priests of the high places that burn incense upon thee, and men’s bones shall be burnt upon thee.

3And he gave a sign the same day, saying, This is the sign which the LORD hath spoken; Behold, the altar shall be rent, and the ashes that are upon it shall be poured out.

4And it came to pass, when king Jeroboam heard the saying of the man of God, which had cried against the altar in Bethel, that he put forth his hand from the altar, saying, Lay hold on him. And his hand, which he put forth toward the man, dried up, so that he could not pull it in again to him.

5The altar also was rent, and the ashes poured out from the altar, according to the sign which the man of God had given by the word of the LORD.

1 Kings 13:1–5 · King James Version
Who was the word addressed to?

Not to Jeroboam, though the king was standing right there. “He cried against the altar… and said, O altar, altar, thus saith the LORD.”

By what authority did he cry?

“In the word of the LORD” (v.1, v.2) and “by the word of the LORD” (v.5). The phrase is repeated four times in five verses. The power was in the word he was given, not in his volume.

What happened to the altar, and when?

Verse 3 promised the altar would be rent and the ashes poured out. Verse 5 records it happened the same day. The long-range word about Josiah (v.2) was confirmed by an immediate sign.

Study Note — the hand that stretched out against the messenger

Jeroboam stretched out his hand to seize the man of God and his hand dried up (v.4). Later in the chapter the king asks the man of God to pray, and the hand is restored (1 Kings 13:6).

Prayer Point 10

“I cry against every altar functioned against my life. O altar! O altar break in pieces in Jesus’ name — 1 Kings 13:2.”

Word Study

Ten words and phrases from these Scriptures, in their plain King James sense.

Study Note — reference material
AltarA built place of sacrifice and dedication. In these passages altars are thrown down (Judges 6:25), repaired (1 Kings 18:30), rent (1 Kings 13:5), and cried against (1 Kings 13:2).
GroveThe wooden object or planting that stood beside Baal’s altar. Gideon was told to cut it down and burn its wood on the LORD’s altar (Judges 6:25–26).
The ordered placeJudges 6:26 — the altar to the LORD was not put anywhere convenient. It was built on the rock, in the arranged, appointed place.
Repaired1 Kings 18:30 — restored what already existed and had fallen into ruin, rather than starting something new.
Broken downDescribes the LORD’s altar in 1 Kings 18:30 — not destroyed by an enemy in battle, but left in disrepair.
DagonThe Philistine god whose house in Ashdod received the captured ark. He fell twice, and the second time his head and palms were cut off (1 Samuel 5:2–4).
ThresholdThe doorway of Dagon’s house, where his head and hands were found broken (1 Samuel 5:4–5).
Cried against1 Kings 13:2 — a public, spoken declaration delivered directly at the altar, in the word of the LORD.
RentTorn apart. 1 Kings 13:5 — “The altar also was rent, and the ashes poured out.”
Possess their possessionsObadiah 1:17 — to take back into ownership what already belongs to the house of Jacob.
