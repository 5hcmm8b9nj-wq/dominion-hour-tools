This message argues that Scripture sometimes reveals troubling patterns in individuals, families, and even cities, and that believers should respond with repentance, discernment, and confidence in the redeeming blood of Jesus.

Study aim 1

Observe repeated patterns such as barrenness, premature death, instability, and blood-linked judgment in the cited passages.

Study aim 2

Distinguish between direct biblical statements and interpretive ministry applications so teaching stays careful and clear.

Use the text prayerfully and responsibly. Some points in the message are explicit in Scripture, while others are ministry inferences drawn from patterns across passages.

Interpretive questions

What is clearly stated in the text?

The passages directly state events such as Haran’s death, barrenness in the family line, Reuben’s sin, and the deaths connected to Jericho’s rebuilding.

What is interpretive application?

Calling these events an “evil foundation” is a ministry interpretation that connects repeated patterns and covenant themes across passages.

How should this affect prayer?

It should move believers toward repentance, renunciation of sin and idols, and fresh trust in the redeeming work of Christ.

Study notes

1. Adam and the first broken foundation

The message begins with Adam because the fall introduces the broadest human foundation problem: sin, sorrow, toil, and death.

2. Terah’s line and recurring patterns

Genesis 11:27–32 introduces a family through which God will work, yet the passage already contains death, delay, and barrenness.

3. Reuben as a warning

Reuben shows that inheritance is not the only issue; personal sin and instability can deepen what is already damaged.

4. Jericho and blood-linked rebuilding

Jericho highlights how a place under judgment can become a vivid example of a troubled foundation.

5. Christ the new foundation

The study reaches its goal only when it lands on redemption: Jesus Christ breaks curses, cleanses sin, and establishes a new covenant foundation.

Genesis 11:27–32 (KJV)

27 Now these are the generations of Terah: Terah begat Abram, Nahor, and Haran; and Haran begat Lot. 28 And Haran died before his father Terah in the land of his nativity, in Ur of the Chaldees. 29 And Abram and Nahor took them wives: the name of Abram's wife was Sarai; and the name of Nahor's wife, Milcah, the daughter of Haran, the father of Milcah, and the father of Iscah. 30 But Sarai was barren; she had no child. 31 And Terah took Abram his son, and Lot the son of Haran his son's son, and Sarai his daughter in law, his son Abram's wife; and they went forth with them from Ur of the Chaldees, to go into the land of Canaan; and they came unto Haran, and dwelt there. 32 And the days of Terah were two hundred and five years: and Terah died in Haran.

Genesis 25:21 (KJV)

And Isaac intreated the LORD for his wife, because she was barren: and the LORD was intreated of him, and Rebekah his wife conceived.

And when the LORD saw that Leah was hated, he opened her womb: but Rachel was barren.

Genesis 35:22 (KJV)

And it came to pass, when Israel dwelt in that land, that Reuben went and lay with Bilhah his father's concubine: and Israel heard it. Now the sons of Jacob were twelve.

Genesis 49:3–4 (KJV)
