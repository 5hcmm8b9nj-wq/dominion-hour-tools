Foundation

What an Altar Really Is

Before we can defeat demonic altars, we must understand what an altar actually is in the spirit realm.

Key Truth

An altar is a place where destinies are decided. An altar is where covenants are made and maintained. There must be an altar for a destiny to be a destiny.

An altar can lift your destiny — or redirect your entire life. Every altar speaks, even after the person who raised it is long gone.

Exodus 20:24–25 (KJV)

“An altar of earth thou shalt make unto me, and shalt sacrifice thereon thy burnt offerings, and thy peace offerings, thy sheep, and thine oxen: in all places where I record my name I will come unto thee, and I will bless thee.”

Warning

Altars can be polluted. Your altar can be polluted; it can be attacked. Today, let the Lord revive your altar.

How the Spirit Realm Works

The Authorization System & the Bridge

Whether you are aware of it or not, everyone has an altar. Altars are a system of authorization. Man is the only being with the legal right to communicate with both the spiritual and the physical realm.

A spirit cannot descend to work on earth without an altar. You give permission to the good Spirit if you are a child of God; you give permission to wicked spirits if you serve evil knowingly or unknowingly.

This is why a person can be born again and still not see God’s presence operating in their life — there is a protocol in the spirit realm.

The Altar as a Bridge

An altar is a spiritual foundation — a platform where the realm of the spirit makes contact with the physical realm on legal grounds. Prayers ascend as incense; fire and angels descend.

Elijah’s Pattern

Repairing the Broken Altar

Elijah repaired the altar of the LORD that was broken down — and so must we.

1 Kings 18:30 (KJV)

“And Elijah said unto all the people, Come near unto me. And all the people came near unto him. And he repaired the altar of the LORD that was broken down.”

The altar had been torn down. The prophet of God repaired the altar. The reason your fire has gone down — the reason your love for God has gone down — is the result of a broken altar.

Today, by the mercy of God, let the altar be repaired. Your prayer time, your time of worship, your time in the Word — it must be repaired. Otherwise the fire will not come down.

Twelve Stones · One Trench · One Sacrifice

  1. Twelve stones — according to the tribes of Israel. Build in the name of the Lord.
  2. One trench — set apart what belongs to God.
  3. One sacrifice — wood in order, the offering laid down.
  4. Water poured three times — leave no room for human credit.
  5. Prayer at the evening sacrifice — God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, answer me.
  6. The fire falls — and consumes the offering, the wood, the stones, and the water.

A Place in Your Home

Setting Up Your Personal Altar

A practical guide to building a place in your home where the fire never goes out.

On Your Altar

  • Bible — the Word at the center.
  • Anointing oil — consecrated for prayer and dedication.
  • Holy Communion — the bread and the cup, in remembrance of the body and blood of Jesus (Luke 22:19–20; 1 Corinthians 11:23–26).
  • Candle or lamp — a reminder that the fire must keep burning.
  • Salt — a symbol of preservation and covenant.
  • Prayer journal & pen — to record what the Lord speaks.

Around Your Altar

  • Comfortable chair — a quiet place to sit, kneel, and stay a while.
  • Quiet corner — set apart from the noise of the house.
  • Soft lamp — for reading the Word during early-morning prayer.
  • Small plant — a living reminder of growth and life.
  • Worship music — soft gospel or instrumental worship.
  • Prayer shawl — a covering set apart for worship.

Things to Do

  • Choose a quiet corner, spare room, or even a garden spot.
  • Place a small table or shelf there — this is your altar.
  • Lay your Bible, anointing oil, prayer journal, and pen on it.
  • Add a candle or lamp, the elements of Holy Communion, and salt as a symbol of preservation.
  • Bring in a comfortable chair so you can stay a while.
  • Pour anointing oil and declare: “Lord, this is Your altar in my house.”
  • Keep the place clean — no clutter, no distracting electronics.

At the Altar

Holy Communion

The bread and the cup are not a ritual — they are a living encounter with the body and the blood of Jesus at your personal altar.

Biblical Foundation

Communion was instituted by the Lord Jesus Himself on the night He was betrayed. He gave the bread and the cup, and commanded us to keep doing this “in remembrance of Me.” It is the New Covenant meal — a covenant sealed in His own blood.

Luke 22:19–20 (KJV)

“And he took bread, and gave thanks, and brake it, and gave unto them, saying, This is my body which is given for you: this do in remembrance of me. Likewise also the cup after supper, saying, This cup is the new testament in my blood, which is shed for you.”

1 Corinthians 11:23–26 (KJV)

“For as often as ye eat this bread, and drink this cup, ye do shew the Lord’s death till he come.”

1 Corinthians 10:16 (KJV)

“The cup of blessing which we bless, is it not the communion of the blood of Christ? The bread which we break, is it not the communion of the body of Christ?”

The Bread and the Cup in Covenant

When you take Communion at your altar, you are not performing a religious act — you are stepping into the everlasting covenant. Each element carries covenant meaning:

The Bread — His Body

  • Healing — “By His stripes we are healed” (Isaiah 53:5; 1 Peter 2:24). The broken body of Jesus carries our wholeness.
  • Strength — the Bread of Life sustains the inner man (John 6:51).
  • One body — we partake of one bread, joined to Christ and His Church (1 Corinthians 10:17).

The Cup — His Blood

  • Forgiveness — “shed for many for the remission of sins” (Matthew 26:28).
  • Covenant — the cup is the New Testament in His blood (Luke 22:20).
  • Cleansing — the blood of Jesus cleanses us from all sin (1 John 1:7).
  • Victory — we overcome by the blood of the Lamb (Revelation 12:11).
Before You Partake

“Let a man examine himself, and so let him eat of that bread, and drink of that cup” (1 Corinthians 11:28). Take a moment to repent of known sin, forgive anyone who has hurt you, and receive the body and blood with reverence and faith.

A Guided Prayer for Communion

Set the bread and the cup on your altar. Quiet your heart. Then pray these words aloud, allowing the Holy Spirit to lead you.

Step 1 · Approach

Heavenly Father, I come before You in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ. I thank You for the privilege of meeting with You at this altar, and for the body and blood of Your Son who has opened the way for me to come boldly into Your presence.

Step 2 · Examine & Repent

Holy Spirit, search my heart. Show me anything that is not pleasing to You. I confess every sin I have committed in thought, word, and deed. I receive Your forgiveness and cleansing by the blood of Jesus (1 John 1:9). I release every person who has wronged me — I forgive as I have been forgiven.

Step 3 · The Bread

(Lift the bread.) Lord Jesus, this bread represents Your body, broken for me. Thank You for bearing my sicknesses and carrying my pains. As I receive this bread, I receive Your healing, Your strength, and Your wholeness into every part of my body, soul, and spirit. (Eat the bread.)

Step 4 · The Cup

(Lift the cup.) Lord Jesus, this cup is the New Covenant in Your blood, shed for the remission of my sins. By Your blood I am redeemed, forgiven, cleansed, and made righteous. Every curse is broken, every accusation is silenced, every demonic claim against my life is annulled by the blood of the Lamb. (Drink the cup.)

Step 5 · Apply & Decree

By the body and the blood I have just received, I decree that the fire on my altar shall keep burning. The blood speaks better things over my life, my family, and my bloodline (Hebrews 12:24). No weapon formed against me shall prosper. I walk in the covenant, in the healing, and in the victory of Jesus Christ.

Step 6 · Thanksgiving

Thank You, Father, for this holy meal. Thank You, Lord Jesus, for Your finished work. Thank You, Holy Spirit, for sealing this covenant in my life. I receive afresh, and I go forth in the strength of this Communion. In the mighty name of Jesus, amen.

Walk It Out

Your 21-Day Altar Assignment

Teaching without action is information. Take these next twenty-one days and walk the teaching out.

Week 1

Build the Altar

Days 1–7

  1. Choose your place — quiet corner, spare room, or garden spot.
  2. Set up the altar — small table, comfortable chair, lamp.
  3. Pour anointing oil and dedicate the place to the Lord.
  4. Place your Bible, prayer journal, pen, and a candle on the altar.
  5. Pray every morning and evening for the first 7 days.
  6. Journal what the Lord says each session.
Week 2

Fortify the Altar

Days 8–14

  1. Begin keeping at least 2 prayer watches (5 AM and 3 PM).
  2. Add fasting — choose one day a week for a 12-hour fast.
  3. Read the prophetic declarations aloud every morning.
  4. Anoint your home, your doorposts, and your bed weekly.
  5. Pray against altars in your father’s and mother’s house.
Week 3

Release Seeds & Harvest

Days 15–21

  1. Release a specific seed — a sacrifice that costs you something.
  2. Write your covenant request and place it on your altar.
  3. Sow into the man or woman of God who covers you spiritually.
  4. Pray the Revelation 8 prayer — let the angel cast fire on your altar.
  5. Write down every answered prayer, dream, and prophetic sight.
  6. On day 21, give a thanksgiving offering and testify.
