Beginner's guide — building a ministry-school syllabus

You have never done this before. That is fine. Every school starts here.

1. What is a syllabus?

A syllabus is the official teaching plan of a school. It is the "map" a student receives on Day 1 and answers seven questions:

QuestionWhat goes there
What is this school?Vision, mission, anchor scripture
Who is it for?Target student, entry requirements
How long is it?Program length (weeks / months / terms)
What will I learn?List of courses (subjects)
What is inside each course?Modules and lessons inside that course
What must I do?Assignments, reading, practicum, exams
What do I get at the end?Certificate, impartation, commissioning

2. The five-course blueprint you are using

The school follows a proven pattern used by many established Christian ministry schools: five compulsory courses, delivered over a fixed term, with a two-fold structure of mentorship and impartation. The five course slots are Foundations & the Spirit, Leadership & Character, Ministry & Warfare, Personal Transformation, and Kingdom Stewardship. Each course also expects four Ordinances of Intimacy: strategic prayer, systemic Word study, worship, and the corporate gathering of believers.

3. Five decisions you must get from the pastors before publishing

  1. Program length — Foundations 12 weeks, Diploma 24 weeks, or Full School 52 weeks?
  2. Meeting cadence — how many classes per week and at what time?
  3. Intake — who can register, how do they apply, is there a screening interview?
  4. Assessment — how does a student pass? (weekly workbook, quizzes, practicum log, oral exam?)
  5. Certificate + Impartation — who signs the certificate, is there a graduation night, is there a laying-on-of-hands service?

You can publish a draft syllabus with these marked "To be confirmed by the pastor" — that is exactly what this site does.

4. The four Ordinances of Intimacy — non-negotiable

Every student, every course, every week, must show evidence of:

  1. Strategic and systemic prayer life
  2. Systemic Word study — KJV, in full, no snippets
  3. Worship — private and corporate
  4. Corporate gathering of believers

This is the first page of every student workbook.

5. Skills you should build on the way

Christian sources only. In this order:

  1. A short course on how to study the Bible using three methods — book-by-book, chapter-by-chapter, and topical.
  2. A curriculum for spiritual growth — the sequence Foundation-level students need before ministry work.
  3. A discipleship framework — the three-part Great Commission that becomes the school's three streams.

6. How to write a learning objective

Every module needs one sentence that finishes: "By the end of this lesson, the student will be able to ______." The verb must be measurable — Explain, Recite, Pray, Demonstrate, Break, Renounce, List — not "understand" or "know," because those cannot be tested.

7. Your build order — do it in this sequence

  1. Confirm the five decisions above with each pastor.
  2. Write the module template (already done — see Module 1).
  3. Convert the existing teachings you already have into the module template — one at a time.
  4. Write the assessment rubric (already done — see rubric).
  5. Design the certificate — one for each school.
  6. Set a first-cohort start date and open registration.
